The framework

The five gates.

Sequential. A product that fails one gate is not assessed against the next — there is no overall score and no partial credit.

0/4
Gate 0
Formulation
Is the ingredient form and dose backed by real evidence?
Checked against a 101-ingredient evidence file (forms, safe dose ranges).
Validated — κ = 0.297, 68% agreement (n=806)
1/4
Gate 1
Regulatory
Is it legal to sell in the UK today?
Checked live against the UK FSA Novel Food register.
Validated — κ = 0.412, 96.5% agreement (n=766) — strongest result
2/4
Gate 2
Quality
Does independent lab testing confirm the label claims?
No open dataset of batch-specific lab certificates exists.
Built, not empirically validated — no public data exists
3/4
Gate 3
Brand Integrity
Is the company real, clean of recent alerts?
Checked live against UK Companies House and FSA Food Alerts.
Validated — 91-93% agreement, weak κ (class imbalance)
4/4
Gate 4
Medical
Does the dose match what clinical studies used?
Uses a safety-threshold proxy — the real cited-study database doesn't exist yet.
Validated — κ = 0.108, known scope limitation
A plain database lookup outperformed every AI-assisted reading approach we tested — even after several rounds of fixing the reading step. Where a question can be answered by checking a real database directly, that beat asking a model to read and decide, every time.
Real findings

What the evidence actually shows.

Lookup > LLM
The gate needing no AI reading step (Gate 1, live database lookup) scored highest. Every AI-assisted extraction gate scored lower, even after multiple rounds of fixes.
Cheap forms, pushed harder
Magnesium oxide (the rejected form) was dosed highest and blended most across the real market data pulled for this project. Premium forms were dosed conservatively and sold with less blending.
