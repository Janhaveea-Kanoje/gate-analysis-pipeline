Accuracy & validation

Every number here comes from comparing the automated pipeline against manually-reviewed real products — not a single one is estimated or assumed.

Why kappa, not just percentage agreement? A raw agreement percentage can look strong purely by chance — if 9 out of 10 real products should PASS, a check that blindly guesses PASS every time already "agrees" 90% of the time without checking anything. Cohen's kappa corrects for this by measuring agreement beyond what chance alone would produce. Every result below reports both, and a majority-class baseline is given wherever it changes how a result should be read.

Gate 3 — why both sub-checks score below chance

Gate 3 has two live checks — Regulatory History (FSA Food Alerts) and Full Disclosure (UK Companies House) — and both post a negative kappa despite agreement over 90%. This isn't a bug; it's what happens when a ground truth is heavily skewed toward one answer.

Regulatory History

91.4% agreement, κ = -0.033 (n=280)

Checks brand-level FSA alerts, not product-level — and doesn't cover ASA advertising-claim rulings, a real scope gap in what "regulatory history" actually means.

Full Disclosure

93.2% agreement, κ = -0.013 (n=281)

Only verifies the company exists on Companies House — the framework's actual "Full Disclosure" requirement is broader than company existence alone.

In both cases, the manual ground truth is 91-98% one answer. A check that always guessed the majority answer would already score close to these percentages without checking anything — which is exactly why kappa, not agreement alone, is the number that matters here.

Gate 4 — before and after fixing extraction

Notice the majority-class baseline (88.4%) beats the raw agreement of both real versions. This is exactly why kappa is reported everywhere in this project — a high percentage next to a low kappa means the check isn't actually adding much insight over just guessing the common answer.

A second correction applied here too: the same null-handling defect found in Gate 0 was also present in Gate 4's validation script. Fixing it raised the reported result from 67.5%/κ=0.108 to 75.9%/κ=0.153 (n=145) — the figure shown above is the corrected one.

A real, unplanned finding: the magnesium market

Pulled directly from real market data during Gate 0's validation work — not something this project set out to find.

Magnesium oxide — the form Gate 0 rejects outright on bioavailability grounds — is both the most heavily blended (88% of products combine it with at least one other form) and the most aggressively dosed (median 320mg) of the five forms studied. Magnesium L-Threonate, a costlier, better-absorbed form, is dosed lowest (144mg) and blended least (21%). Cheaper, lower-bioavailability forms pushed harder; costlier, better forms sold conservatively — a pattern this project didn't go looking for.

Built and demonstrated, not yet accuracy-tested

Two real capabilities exist in the pipeline with no ground-truth column to test them against, so they're reported honestly as demonstrated rather than validated: Formulation Logic (Gate 0's multi-ingredient contraindication check — proven on a real Copper+Zinc case) and drug-interaction checking (Gate 4's Population Safety check against 334 real DDInter records — proven on a real Magnesium Citrate + Dolutegravir case). Both are live, working code; neither has a number above because no manually-labelled data exists to measure them against.
